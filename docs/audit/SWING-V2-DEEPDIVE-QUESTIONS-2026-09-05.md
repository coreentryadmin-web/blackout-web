### State machine & concurrency

**1. In src/app/api/cron/swing-discovery/route.ts (GET handler, ~lines 289-334), the `force=1` path does `await sharedCacheDel(decision.key)` unconditionally whenever `force` is set — not only when the existing claim is provably stale/expired — and then immediately re-acquires the same `(sessionDay, phase)` key via `sharedCacheSetNx`. Concretely: the natural 16:15 ET POST_CLOSE EventBridge fire acquires the running claim and is 90s into a genuinely healthy (not hung) scan when an ops-recovery `?force=1` call (or an automated retry script) fires. That call deletes the still-live 'running' claim out from under the in-flight scan and starts a second `runSwingDiscoveryScan` for the identical (date, phase) key. Both invocations independently call `fetchOpenBook()` before either one's `insertPosition` has landed, so both pass the `already_open` thesisKey check (commit.ts ~line 319) for the same (ticker, direction, archetype). If the two near-simultaneous `fetchChainRows` calls pick contracts in different DTE buckets, the resulting `subLane` — and therefore `commit_key` (commit.ts line 286) — differs between the two decisions, so `insertSwingPosition`'s `ON CONFLICT (commit_key)` upsert (db.ts ~line 7544) does NOT collapse them: two distinct real positions open for one thesis. Is there any guard preventing `force=1` from tearing down a claim that a legitimately-running (non-hung) invocation still holds, or is the idempotency here silently relying on the two commit_keys happening to match?**
> Citation: src/app/api/cron/swing-discovery/route.ts lines 289-334 (force/claim logic); src/lib/swing/commit.ts lines 278-362 (openThesis/commitKey); src/lib/db.ts lines 7518-7548 (insertSwingPosition upsert on commit_key)
>
> Why it matters: This is a real-money double-open path: the idempotency gate (thesisKey) and the DB upsert key (commit_key) are two DIFFERENT identities, and the force-recovery mechanism can create exactly the two-concurrent-invocation condition that makes the gap between those two identities matter.

**2. `deriveSwingCandidates` (discovery.ts lines 241-245) is a bare `.map((s) => buildSwingDossier(s.input))` over every Tier-1-enriched candidate seed, and `mapPool` (lines 154-169) has no per-item try/catch around `await fn(items[idx], idx)` — a rejecting promise from ANY one of the (up to 8) concurrent `enrichCandidate` calls propagates through the shared `Promise.all` and aborts every sibling worker too. Given `buildSwingDossier` calls into `classifyArchetype`, `scoreSwingPillars`, and several pillar helpers on data assembled from five-plus independently fail-soft provider reads, is it actually guaranteed today that NONE of those pure functions can throw on a malformed-but-not-null input for any of up to 40 names? If one can throw, a single poisoned candidate discards every OTHER already-enriched candidate's dossier for the entire scan, propagates uncaught into route.ts's catch, releases the phase claim — and if the trigger is a deterministic data shape for that ticker, a `?force=1` retry of the SAME phase will hit the identical throw again, permanently wedging that day's phase.**
> Citation: src/lib/swing/discovery.ts lines 154-169 (mapPool, no per-item isolation), lines 241-245 (deriveSwingCandidates bare .map); src/lib/swing/dossier.ts lines 133-183 (buildSwingDossier)
>
> Why it matters: Every other enrichment reader in this pipeline is explicitly fail-soft (`.catch(() => null)`) specifically so one bad name never sinks the scan — but the pure scoring/classification stage that runs AFTER all that fail-soft IO has no equivalent per-candidate isolation, making it the one place a single-candidate failure can take down the entire batch.

**3. `watchCandidates`, `observedCandidates`, and `playSet` are all computed in discovery.ts (lines 693-726) BEFORE the 'LIVE COMMIT' block (lines 761+) ever reads `fetchOpenBook()` or executes `computeSwingCommitPlan`/`executeSwingCommits`. > Why it matters: WATCH and COMMITTED are two different states this codebase clearly distinguishes internally, but the member-facing serving snapshot never reconciles them back together — so the desk's own state machine can silently desync from what members are shown, on the very scan where the transition happens.
