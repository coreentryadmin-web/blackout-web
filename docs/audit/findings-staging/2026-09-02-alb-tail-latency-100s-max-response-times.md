## 2026-09-02 — [FINDING, P2 infra/performance] Production ALB `TargetResponseTime` Max repeatedly hits 90-119s (idle-timeout territory) — 57 of the last 80 fifteen-minute windows exceeded 20s — OPEN, write-up only, per the new standing performance mandate

> **kind:** `FINDING`

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What prompted this** | First application of `CLAUDE.md`'s new "STANDING PERFORMANCE/LATENCY AUDIT MANDATE" (added 2026-09-02, PR #3296) — its own prescribed first step is "measure before guessing": pull `AWS/ApplicationELB` `TargetResponseTime` (p50/p90/p99/Max) on `blackout-production-app`'s target group. Did exactly that, over a 20-hour window, rather than assuming the site "feels fast" from p50 alone. |
| **The measurement** | `cloudwatch.get_metric_statistics(Namespace='AWS/ApplicationELB', MetricName='TargetResponseTime', ...)` at 15-min (900s) granularity, 2026-09-01 09:48 UTC → 2026-09-02 03:03 UTC (80 windows). **p50 is genuinely excellent throughout — 50-70ms** (confirmed in the most recent 12 windows: p50 ranged 0.049-0.070s). But **57 of 80 windows (71%) had a Max exceeding 20 seconds**, and the majority of those were far worse: dozens of windows hit **90-119 seconds** — repeatedly, hour after hour, not a one-off. A representative sample from the tail: `11:03→118.6s`, `13:33→118.3s`, `14:03→118.8s`, `15:18→119.0s`, `18:48→114.7s`, `19:48→118.6s`, `21:03→111.2s`, `03:03→100.0s` (today). This is a severe tail-latency problem hiding underneath an excellent average — exactly the shape the mandate's own text calls out as needing a different fix than a uniformly-slow fleet. |
| **The 90-119s ceiling is not random — it sits just under the ALB's own 120s idle timeout** | `elb.describe_load_balancer_attributes` confirms `idle_timeout.timeout_seconds = 120` on `blackout-production-alb`. Nearly every severe spike lands in the 90-119s band, never meaningfully above 120s — consistent with requests being held open until something (the idle timeout, a client, or the target) finally cuts them off, rather than a request that is simply "slow but working." A response that completes at 118.8s on a 120s idle timeout reads as a request that nearly timed out, not one that took that long by design. |
| **Two plausible causes MEASURED and RULED OUT — not guessed away** | Per the mandate's own rigor requirement, checked the two most obvious "something is just slow" explanations directly rather than assuming: **(1) Largo AI tool-loop turns** — `[largo/turn-phases]` logs carry a `total_ms` field; pulled every occurrence in a 6h window: max was **14.5s**, nowhere near 90-100s. Ruled out. **(2) Cron/background jobs** — CloudWatch `elapsed=`/`elapsedMs=` log lines around three of the worst spike windows (02:46, 03:01, 02:31 UTC today) showed only `[cron/platform-warm]` entries at **8-79ms**. Ruled out. Neither the AI chat path nor the background-cron path is where these seconds are going. |
| **What lines up instead: deploy frequency and rolling-deployment mechanics** | This repo redeploys on essentially every merge to `main` (`ecr-push-production.yml`), and merge velocity this session alone has been roughly one every 20-90 minutes during active hours — closely matching the 09:00-22:00 UTC window where nearly every 15-min bucket shows a spike, versus the visibly calmer 22:00-01:00 UTC window (`22:18→(none)`, `00:33→(none)`, `01:03→(none)` — no >20s max at all in several consecutive windows) before ramping back up as soon as this session's own merges resumed at 02:33+. The target group's own settings plausibly contribute a multi-tens-of-seconds warmup/drain cost per deploy: `HealthCheckIntervalSeconds=30` × `HealthyThresholdCount=2` means a **freshly-started task needs up to ~60-70s of passing health checks before it's eligible for traffic**, and `deregistration_delay.timeout_seconds=30` gives an **old task 30s to finish in-flight requests** before the ALB stops routing to it and it's force-killed — two windows that, stacked or landed on back-to-back deploys (a real possibility at this merge cadence), could plausibly account for a request stuck in the 90-120s range. This is a plausible mechanism, not yet a proven one — see below. |
| **Why not fixed directly this cycle — genuinely can't close the loop from here** | Confirming which of these mechanisms (or something else entirely) is the actual cause needs **per-request attribution this sandbox cannot get today**: `elb.describe_load_balancer_attributes` confirms `access_logs.s3.enabled = false` — there is no ALB access log to grep for the specific URI, target, and elapsed time behind any one of these 90-119s outliers. Without that, correlating a specific spike to a specific deploy's specific health-check/drain window is circumstantial (timing pattern + magnitude match), not proven. This is squarely infra-configuration territory (ALB/target-group settings, ECS deployment `minimumHealthyPercent`/`maximumPercent`, possibly Next.js's own SIGTERM graceful-shutdown handling for in-flight requests) — exactly the kind of change `CLAUDE.md`'s own standing terraform-drift caution says to make as one surgical, read-first `aws`/boto3 call rather than guess through blind, and a decision (is 30s deregistration delay actually enough given real request latencies; should ALB access logging be turned on to get definitive attribution) that belongs to whoever owns the ALB/ECS deploy pipeline. |
| **Suggested next steps for whoever picks this up** | (1) **Enable ALB access logging to an S3 bucket** (a safe, additive, reversible attribute flip — `access_logs.s3.enabled=true` + a bucket) — this is the single highest-leverage next step, since it turns "which request, which target, how long" from a mystery into a two-minute log query. (2) Once attributed, check whether the slow requests are landing on tasks in their health-check warmup window, their deregistration-drain window, or neither — that determines whether the fix is tuning `deregistration_delay`/health-check cadence, adding a Next.js graceful-shutdown drain handler for in-flight requests on SIGTERM, or something unrelated to deploys entirely. (3) If deploy-correlated, weigh the deploy cadence itself — the current very-high merge-to-deploy ratio may be worth a "batch merges before deploying" or "widen the ECS deployment's `minimumHealthyPercent`" conversation, since deploy frequency multiplies however much tail-latency risk each individual deploy carries. |
| **Status** | OPEN — measured, quantified, two candidate causes ruled out with evidence, one plausible mechanism identified but not proven without ALB access-log attribution this sandbox can't enable unilaterally (a real infra spend/ownership decision); flagged for whoever owns the ALB/ECS deploy pipeline. No infra change made this cycle. |
